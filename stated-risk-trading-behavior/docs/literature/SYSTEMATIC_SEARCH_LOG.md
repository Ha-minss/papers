# Structured Novelty Search Log

**Search date:** 2026-08-01  
**Research question:** Do observed MiFID-style risk profiles provide incremental out-of-time predictive value beyond revealed trading behavior for account-level responses to repeated security-specific extreme negative-return events?

## Sources searched

Publicly accessible publisher pages and indices were searched through two web search systems, including Elsevier/ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, Wiley Online Library, SSRN, arXiv, RePEc/IDEAS, institutional research repositories, and the FAR-Trans repository/paper trail.

**Not directly searched behind institutional login:** Scopus, Web of Science, and ProQuest Dissertations & Theses. The novelty statement therefore does not use “first study” language.

## Inclusion criteria

- Peer-reviewed article, working paper, conference paper, or official dataset paper.
- Uses at least one of: elicited risk/perceptions, actual account-level trading, crisis/extreme-return response, or predictive investor modelling.
- Provides enough method detail to compare the unit of analysis, event design, outcome, and validation.

## Exclusion criteria

- Pure asset-price prediction without investor-level behavior.
- Hypothetical portfolio allocation only, unless directly relevant to risk-questionnaire validity.
- Institutional-only order flow without retail account profiles.
- News/recommendation papers that do not test risk profiles or stress responses.

## Search result

No publicly indexed study located in this search combines all four design elements below:

1. observed MiFID-style customer risk categories;
2. actual account-level securities transactions;
3. repeated security-specific extreme negative-return events with Buy/Sell/NoAction outcomes; and
4. chronological out-of-time comparison of stated profiles against lagged revealed behavior.

Several close studies cover two or three elements. They are catalogued in `EVIDENCE_MATRIX.csv`. This supports a narrow extension claim, not a universal priority claim.

## Safe novelty statement

> We extend prior survey-, portfolio-, and crisis-based evidence by evaluating whether observed MiFID risk profiles provide incremental out-of-time predictive value beyond revealed trading behavior for account-level responses to repeated security-specific extreme negative-return events.

## Residual novelty risk

- A similar proprietary-bank study may exist but be unpublished or poorly indexed.
- Scopus, Web of Science, and ProQuest searches could identify additional adjacent work.
- The main contribution should therefore be framed as the exact design and comparison, not as the first study of risk tolerance and trading behavior.
