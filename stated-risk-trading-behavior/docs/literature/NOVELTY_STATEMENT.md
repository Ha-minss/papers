# Novelty Position

## What is not new

- Relating risk tolerance or investor perceptions to actual trading.
- Comparing elicited risk with portfolio risk.
- Studying retail trading around crises or extreme returns.
- Predicting retail trading from account history.
- Using FAR-Trans for financial-asset research.

## What this paper adds

The paper places stated profiles and revealed behavior in a direct, chronological predictive contest. It constructs repeated security-specific extreme negative-return exposures, observes the first Buy/Sell/NoAction response for holders, and asks whether MiFID-style risk categories improve prediction after strictly lagged transaction and prior-event-response features are available.

## Defensible contribution language

> We extend prior survey-, portfolio-, and crisis-based evidence by evaluating whether observed MiFID risk profiles provide incremental out-of-time predictive value beyond revealed trading behavior for account-level responses to repeated security-specific extreme negative-return events.

## Language to avoid

- “This is the first study of risk tolerance during market shocks.”
- “MiFID profiles are invalid.”
- “Price shocks cause customers to buy or sell.”
- “Prior selling reveals panic.”

## Current evidence boundary

The structured public search was completed on 2026-08-01. Scopus, Web of Science, and ProQuest were not directly queried behind an institutional login. The manuscript therefore makes a narrow extension claim rather than a universal first-study claim.
